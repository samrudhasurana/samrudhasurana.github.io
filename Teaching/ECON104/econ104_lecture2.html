Gains from Specialization

  • We don’t just have different preferences – we also have different natural abilities, skills, interests, and access to resources.
  • Trade creates value even if the total number of goods doesn’t change.
  • But trade also creates more goods. How? Specialization.
  • Imagine you had to grow your own food, build your own house, and fix everything yourself – what would your cost be? Think in opportunity-cost terms.

ECON 104
08

Adam Smith’s Benefits of Specialization

1

Dexterity

Greater skill from each worker, learned by doing.

2

No switching time

Workers save the time otherwise lost moving between tasks.

3

Invention & machinery

Specialists are more likely to invent and use better machinery.

This idea is the division of labor. Other benefits include economies of scale and the benefits of specialized knowledge.
ECON 104
09

Limited by the Extent of the Market

“This is awesome! Can we indefinitely keep extending this?” No – the division of labor is limited by the extent of the market.

Two Kinds of Advantage

Absolute advantage

The ability to produce the same good using fewer inputs than another producer.

Comparative advantage

The ability to produce a good or service at a lower opportunity cost than another producer.

Setting Up the Example

14

Opportunity Cost

With no trade, each country produces some of each good. The opportunity cost of a good is the amount of the other good given up to get it.

CountryOpportunity Cost of 1 ComputerOpportunity Cost of 1 Shirt
Mexico6 shirts1/6 of a computer
United States1 shirt1 computer
ECON 104
15

Comparative Advantage

Compare the opportunity costs: whoever gives up less of the other good has the comparative advantage.

Mexico has a comparative advantage in shirts, and the United States has a comparative advantage in computers – each has the lower opportunity cost in that good.
ECON 104
16

You Don’t Need an Absolute Advantage

  • To benefit from trade, a country doesn’t need an absolute advantage.
  • A country can benefit from trade if it has a comparative advantage.
  • Comparative advantage can be illustrated with a production possibilities frontier.
Production Possibilities Frontier

All the combinations of goods a country can produce, given its productivity and supply of inputs.

Comparative Advantage and Wages

  • Differences in wages reflect differences in productivity.
  • Wages will be higher in high-productivity countries than in low-productivity countries.
  • Trade raises wages to the highest levels allowed by a country’s productivity.

Trade Is Always Profitable Somewhere

  • What makes trade profitable is differences in comparative advantage.
  • A country will always have some comparative advantage.
  • Thus, everyone can benefit from trade.
