Demand

  • The demand curve shows the quantity demanded at each price.
  • It slopes down.
  • Lower price → buyers buy more; higher price → buyers buy less.
  • A shift in the curve = willing to buy more (or less) at the same price.
ECON 104
04

Supply

  • The supply curve shows the quantity supplied at each price.
  • It slopes up.
  • Lower price → sellers sell less; higher price → sellers sell more.
  • A shift in the curve = willing to sell more (or less) at the same price.

Equilibrium price (“market-clearing price”)

The price at which the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied.

At this price, every unit brought to market is bought — and everyone goes home happy.

Like a ball in a bowl

  • We call these the equilibrium price and quantity for a reason.
  • At any other price or quantity, market forces kick in and push the market back toward them.
  • Like a ball in a bowl — it always rolls back to the bottom.

Out of equilibrium: surpluses

What if the price is $5 — above the equilibrium?

Quantity supplied (500) exceeds quantity demanded (300): a surplus of 200 units.
ECON 104

Out of equilibrium: shortages

What if the price is $2 — below the equilibrium?

Quantity demanded (600) exceeds quantity supplied (200): a shortage of 400 units.
ECON 104
10

Understanding why: look at competition

  • Who competes against whom?
    • Price above equilibrium → sellers compete with sellers, offering lower prices, until the equilibrium price is reached.
    • Price below equilibrium → buyers compete with buyers, offering higher prices, until the equilibrium price is reached.
  • Whenever a shortage or a surplus exists, incentives drive the price toward equilibrium.
Only the equilibrium price is stable.

What’s in a price?

  • Prices convey information about events we have no conception of.
  • Prices let us take advantage of other people’s knowledge…
  • …and give us the incentive to consume less, or supply more, of a good.
  • All without any one person planning it.
Friedrich A. Hayek, “The Use of Knowledge in Society” (1945).

Overview

  • Equilibrium is the price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied — the market-clearing price.
  • Out of equilibrium, a surplus pushes the price down and a shortage pushes it up — competition drives the price back to equilibrium, like a ball rolling back to the bottom of a bowl.
  • At the equilibrium price, every trade that can generate value takes place: consumer and producer surplus together — total surplus — are as large as they can be.
  • A shift in supply or demand changes the equilibrium price and quantity; the direction depends on which curve shifts and which way.
  • Prices convey information, let us use other people’s knowledge, and give us the incentive to consume less or supply more — all without anyone planning it.
