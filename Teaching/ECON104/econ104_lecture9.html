Fact #3: There Are Growth Miracles and Growth Disasters

Growth miracles

  1. The US is one of the wealthiest countries in the world, because it grew slowly but consistently over the last 200 years.
  2. Japan grew at 8.5% for 20 years just after WWII.
    1. At this rate, GDP per capita doubles every 8 years!
  3. South Korea grew at a rate of 7.2% per year between 1970 and 1990.

Growth Disasters

  1. Nigeria has barely grown since 1950 and was actually poorer in 2005 than in 1974 (when high oil prices increased its GDP per capita).
  2. In 1900, Argentina was one of the richest countries in the world with a GDP per capita 75% as large as the United States.
    1. But slow growth rates have caused it to lag — Argentina’s GDP per capita is now 36% as large as the US.
    2. In 1950, Argentina’s GDP per capita was more than double that of Japan and South Korea.
    3. Today Japan and South Korea have GDP per capita ($47,000 and $50,000, respectively) nearly double that of Argentina ($27,000).
ECON 104

Good News and Bad News

  • Many, many people are still poor.
  • But catching up is not impossible.
  • On average, a 1% increase in average income reduces the share of people in extreme poverty by roughly 2%.
    • Less in Sub-Saharan Africa: about 1%.
Sources: Ravallion (2001), “Growth, Inequality and Poverty: Looking Beyond Averages,” World Development; Wu, Atamanov, Bundervoet & Paci (2024), “The Growth Elasticity of Poverty,” World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 10690.
